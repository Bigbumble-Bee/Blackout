BLACKOUT connects Black, Latino, Indigenous, Asian American, immigrant, LGBTQ+, and other community histories with the larger stories around them.

THE TIMELINE

PIECES
OF THE WALL.

1865–1877
RECONSTRUCTION

Building freedom after slavery

Reconstruction brought major changes in citizenship, political participation, voting, and the legal status of formerly enslaved people.

Look at the 13th, 14th, and 15th Amendments, Black political participation, Freedmen's conventions, and the struggles surrounding Reconstruction.
1896
LAW / SEGREGATION

Plessy v. Ferguson

The Supreme Court decision became a major part of the legal history of segregation in the United States.

Compare this case with later legal challenges to segregation, especially Brown v. Board of Education.
1916–1945
GREAT MIGRATION

Millions move.

Large numbers of African Americans moved from the South to cities in other parts of the United States, reshaping communities and culture.

Follow the connections between migration, labor, housing, music, newspapers, politics, and the growth of Black urban communities.
1920s
HARLEM RENAISSANCE

Art becomes an archive.

Literature, music, visual art, performance, journalism, and intellectual life became central to a major period of Black cultural expression.

Connect poetry, jazz, newspapers, photography, literature, migration, and debates about identity.
1954
BROWN v. BOARD

School segregation challenged.

Brown v. Board of Education became a landmark Supreme Court decision in the history of school desegregation.

Research the cases and events that led toward the decision and the resistance that followed it.
1955–1968
CIVIL RIGHTS ERA

Organizing, protest, law, and change

A wide range of organizers, students, lawyers, community groups, churches, and activists challenged racial segregation and discrimination.

Do not reduce this period to a handful of famous names. Study local organizing, youth activism, women organizers, legal challenges, voting-rights work, and community institutions.
MANY ERAS
LATINO HISTORY

Migration + labor + culture

Latino history in the United States includes many different communities, regions, migrations, cultures, political movements, and experiences.

Follow Mexican American, Puerto Rican, Cuban, Dominican, Central American, South American, Caribbean, and other Latino histories rather than treating them as one story.
BEFORE / DURING / AFTER COLONIZATION
INDIGENOUS HISTORY

Nations, sovereignty, survival

Indigenous histories begin long before the creation of the United States and continue into the present.

Study individual nations and communities, sovereignty, treaties, removal, boarding schools, language revitalization, art, land, and contemporary community life.
MANY ERAS
LGBTQ+ HISTORY

People have always made history.

LGBTQ+ history includes changing identities, communities, cultural expression, organizing, discrimination, relationships, art, and politics.

Study specific communities and periods instead of treating LGBTQ+ history as a single event. Stonewall is one part of a much larger history.
