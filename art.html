THE WALL

ART IS
EVERYWHERE.

Street art can be public argument, decoration, memorial, identity, humor, protest, documentation, or several of those things at once.

TAG
GRAFFITI

Letters have bodies.

Look at shape, rhythm, scale, placement, repetition, movement, and the relationship between a tag and its surroundings.

What makes one letter feel heavy? What makes another feel fast?
FRAME
PHOTOGRAPHY

The street remembers.

Photography can preserve murals, neighborhoods, family stories, demonstrations, celebrations, and everyday life.

Ask what is inside the frame — and what the photographer left outside it.
CUT
FILM / MEDIA

Who gets the camera?

Study who is behind the camera, who is represented, who gets to tell the story, and who controls distribution.

Change the camera angle. How does the story change?
ZINE
ZINES

Low budget. High signal.

Photocopies, handwriting, collage, headlines, marginalia, repetition, tape, and intentional mess.

A zine does not need permission to become a document.
WALL
MURALS

THE WALL TALKS.

Murals can transform ordinary walls into community memory, celebration, political commentary, or public art.

Who commissioned it? Who painted it? Who is represented?

ART IS
NOT A COSTUME.

BLACKOUT can borrow visual language from graffiti, zines, album covers, murals, newspapers, and street photography without turning real cultures into caricatures.

The goal is to learn from the work, understand the context, and make something of your own.
